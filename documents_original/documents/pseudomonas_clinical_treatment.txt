Clinical Treatment of Pseudomonas aeruginosa Infections

Introduction
Pseudomonas aeruginosa is a gram-negative, aerobic bacterium notorious for its intrinsic resistance to many antibiotics and its ability to rapidly acquire additional resistance mechanisms. It is a leading cause of hospital-acquired infections, particularly in immunocompromised patients, burn victims, and those with cystic fibrosis. Treating Pseudomonas infections requires careful antibiotic selection based on susceptibility testing due to widespread multidrug resistance.

Clinical Manifestations
Pseudomonas aeruginosa causes a wide range of infections depending on the site of entry and the patient's immune status:
- Respiratory infections: pneumonia, particularly ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP) in ICU patients
- Wound infections: burn wound sepsis, surgical site infections
- Urinary tract infections: especially in catheterized patients
- Bloodstream infections: bacteremia with high mortality rate (30–50%)
- Ear infections: otitis externa ("swimmer's ear") from contaminated water
- Eye infections: bacterial keratitis from contaminated contact lens solution

Antibiotic Treatment

First-Line Agents:
Antipseudomonal beta-lactams are the cornerstone of treatment:
- Piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5g IV every 6 hours — widely used for moderate infections
- Cefepime 2g IV every 8 hours — fourth-generation cephalosporin with good Pseudomonas activity
- Ceftazidime 2g IV every 8 hours — third-generation cephalosporin, classic antipseudomonal agent
- Meropenem 1–2g IV every 8 hours — carbapenem reserved for severe or resistant infections
- Imipenem-cilastatin 500mg IV every 6 hours — alternative carbapenem

Combination Therapy:
For severe infections or suspected MDR Pseudomonas, combination therapy is recommended:
- Beta-lactam + aminoglycoside (gentamicin or tobramycin) — synergistic killing effect
- Beta-lactam + fluoroquinolone (ciprofloxacin 400mg IV every 8 hours) — used when aminoglycoside toxicity is a concern

Newer Agents for MDR/XDR Pseudomonas:
- Ceftolozane-tazobactam — specifically designed for MDR Pseudomonas, highly effective
- Ceftazidime-avibactam — effective against carbapenem-resistant strains
- Imipenem-cilastatin-relebactam — approved for MDR Pseudomonas pneumonia
- Colistin (polymyxin E) — last resort for pan-drug-resistant (PDR) Pseudomonas

Duration of Treatment:
- Uncomplicated urinary tract infection: 7–10 days
- Pneumonia: 10–14 days (may extend to 21 days for bacteremia)
- Bacteremia: minimum 14 days after blood culture clearance
- Bone and joint infections: 4–6 weeks

Resistance Mechanisms
Pseudomonas aeruginosa employs multiple simultaneous resistance strategies:
- Efflux pumps (MexAB-OprM, MexXY-OprM): actively pump antibiotics out of the cell
- Outer membrane porin loss (OprD): reduces carbapenem entry into the cell
- Beta-lactamase production: AmpC, ESBL, and metallo-beta-lactamases (MBL) including VIM and IMP
- Biofilm formation: creates a protective matrix that reduces antibiotic penetration by up to 1000-fold

Susceptibility Testing
Before initiating treatment, culture and sensitivity testing is mandatory. Pseudomonas susceptibility is unpredictable and cannot be assumed. Minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) testing guides dose optimization, particularly for pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic (PK/PD) target attainment.

Supportive Care
- Source control: removal of infected catheters, drainage of abscesses, debridement of infected wounds
- Respiratory support: mechanical ventilation for severe pneumonia
- Nutritional support: high-protein diet for burn patients with Pseudomonas wound infections
- Infection control: strict contact precautions, hand hygiene, environmental decontamination

Prognosis
Outcomes depend heavily on early appropriate antibiotic therapy. Delay of even 24 hours in starting effective treatment significantly increases mortality in Pseudomonas bacteremia. Immunocompromised patients have mortality rates of 40–60% with inadequate treatment versus 20–30% with appropriate early therapy.
