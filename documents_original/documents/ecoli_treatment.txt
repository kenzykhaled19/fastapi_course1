Escherichia coli (E. coli) in Water: Detection, Treatment, and Prevention

Escherichia coli is a Gram-negative, rod-shaped bacterium and one of the most common indicators of fecal contamination in water supplies. While most strains are harmless, pathogenic variants such as E. coli O157:H7 can cause severe gastrointestinal illness, kidney failure, and even death.

## 1. Why E. coli is Used as an Indicator

Water quality testing typically targets E. coli because it is always present in the intestines of warm-blooded animals. Its presence in water signals that fecal matter has entered the supply, meaning other dangerous pathogens such as Salmonella, Vibrio, or Cryptosporidium may also be present.

- **Total Coliforms:** A broader group that includes environmental bacteria.
- **Fecal Coliforms / E. coli:** A specific subset that confirms fecal contamination.
- **Standard Limit:** The WHO guideline states that E. coli should be undetectable (0 CFU per 100 mL) in drinking water.

## 2. Primary Disinfection Methods

### Chlorination
Chlorine is the most widely used chemical disinfectant against E. coli.

- **Mechanism:** Free chlorine (HOCl) penetrates the bacterial cell wall and oxidizes critical enzymes, disrupting metabolism and killing the organism.
- **Effective Dose:** A free chlorine residual of 0.2 to 0.5 mg/L is sufficient to inactivate E. coli within minutes.
- **Limitation:** Chlorine reacts with organic matter in the water to form disinfection by-products (DBPs) such as trihalomethanes (THMs), which are potentially carcinogenic.

### Ultraviolet (UV) Radiation
UV-C radiation at 254 nm is highly effective against E. coli.

- **Mechanism:** UV light penetrates the bacterial cell and causes the formation of thymine dimers in the DNA strand, preventing replication.
- **Advantage:** Leaves no chemical residue in the water and does not alter taste or odor.
- **Limitation:** Requires low turbidity water. Suspended particles can shield bacteria from UV light, reducing effectiveness.

### Ozonation
Ozone (O3) is a powerful oxidant that is more effective than chlorine against E. coli.

- **Mechanism:** Ozone breaks down cell walls and destroys intracellular components through oxidation.
- **Advantage:** Leaves no residual chemical taste and is highly effective at low concentrations.
- **Limitation:** Ozone decomposes quickly and provides no residual disinfection protection after treatment.

### Boiling
Boiling is the most accessible and reliable emergency method.

- **Mechanism:** High heat denatures bacterial proteins and destroys cell membranes.
- **Specifics:** A rolling boil for 1 minute (3 minutes at altitudes above 2000 meters) completely inactivates all E. coli strains, including O157:H7.

## 3. Advanced Treatment Methods

For large-scale municipal treatment, a multi-barrier approach is standard:

| **Stage** | **Purpose** |
| --- | --- |
| Coagulation/Flocculation | Clumps bacteria and particles together using alum or ferric sulfate |
| Sedimentation | Allows clumped particles to settle to the bottom |
| Filtration (Rapid Sand) | Removes remaining particles and attached bacteria |
| Disinfection (Chlorine/UV) | Final kill step to ensure 99.99% inactivation |

## 4. Clinical Treatment (Medical Infections)

For humans infected with pathogenic E. coli through contaminated water:

- **Mild cases:** Oral rehydration therapy (ORT) and rest.
- **Severe cases (O157:H7):** Hospitalization; antibiotics are generally avoided as they can trigger the release of Shiga toxin, worsening hemolytic uremic syndrome (HUS).
- **Urinary tract infections (E. coli UTI):** Treated with Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole or Nitrofurantoin, depending on antibiotic sensitivity.

Note: Antibiotic resistance is increasing among E. coli strains. Clinical diagnosis and sensitivity testing are essential before prescribing any antibiotic.
