PSEUDOMONAS BACTERIA 
1. Introduction & Definition 
Pseudomonas is a genus of Gram-negative, aerobic, non-spore-forming gammaproteobacteria. They are rod-shaped (bacilli) and belong to the family Pseudomonadaceae. These bacteria are ubiquitous in nature, found in soil, water,plants, and animals. 
While the genus contains many species, Pseudomonas aeruginosa is the most clinically significant human pathogen. It is a classic "opportunistic pathogen," meaning it rarely causes disease in healthy individuals but can cause devastating infections in hospitalized or immunocompromised patients. 
2. Morphology & Laboratory Identification Gram Stain: Gram-negative (pink/red rods).
Motility: Highly motile via a single polar flagellum.
Metabolism: Obligate aerobe (uses oxygen as the terminal electronacceptor).
Oxidase & Catalase: Positive for both.
Pigment Production:
Pyocyanin: Blue-green pigment (toxic to host cells).
Pyoverdine: Yellow-green fluorescent pigment (siderophore for ironuptake).
Odor: Produces a characteristic sweet, grape-like or "corn tortilla" odor in culture.
A Comprehensive Medical & Microbiological Profile 1 Culture: Grows well at 37°C and even up to 42°C (distinguishes it from other Pseudomonas species). It is a non-lactose fermenter on MacConkey agar. 3. Virulence Factors & Pathogenesis The pathogenicity of P. aeruginosa is multi-factorial, involving structural components and secreted toxins: 
Adhesins: Pili and flagella help the bacteria attach to host epithelial cells.
Alginate (Capsule): A mucoid exopolysaccharide that allows the formation of Biofilms. This is critical in chronic lung infections like Cystic Fibrosis, as it protects the bacteria from antibiotics and phagocytosis. 
Exotoxin A (ETA): Its most important toxin. It inhibits protein synthesis by inactivating Elongation Factor-2 (EF-2), leading to tissue necrosis. Enzymes: Elastases, Proteases, and Phospholipase C, which break down host tissues and lung surfactants.Type III Secretion System: Injects toxins directly into host cells, causing cell death and suppressing the immune response.
4. Clinical Manifestations Pseudomonas can infect almost any part of the body if the host defenses are breached:
A. Pulmonary Infections A leading cause of Ventilator-Associated Pneumonia (VAP). It causes chronic, life-limiting infections in patients with Cystic Fibrosis.
B. Skin & Soft Tissue Burn Wounds: Colonizes and causes sepsis in severe burn victims. Hot Tub Folliculitis: Skin rash from contaminated recreational water. Ecthyma Gangrenosum: Hemorrhagic, necrotic skin lesions typical of
Pseudomonas bacteremia.
C. Ear & Eye Infections Otitis Externa: "Swimmer's Ear."
Malignant Otitis Externa: Severe invasive infection in diabetic patients.
Bacterial Keratitis: Often associated with contaminated contact lens use.
D. Other Infections Urinary Tract Infections (UTIs) in catheterized patients, Endocarditis in IV drug users, and Hospital-Acquired Bacteremia.
5. Diagnostic Approaches Specimen Collection: Blood, sputum, urine, or tissue biopsy.
Microscopy: Gram stain showing thin Gram-negative bacilli.
Culture: Isolation on Blood Agar (showing metallic sheen and betahemolysis) or MacConkey Agar.
Biochemical Identification: Oxidase positive, glucose oxidative (not fermentative).
Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing (AST): Mandatory due to the high rate of multi-drug resistance (MDR).
6. Treatment & Management Treatment is complicated by high intrinsic and acquired resistance. Never treat a serious infection without susceptibility results.

7. Prevention & Control 
Hospital Hygiene: Hand washing and strict isolation protocols for MDR cases.
Equipment Care: Proper sterilization of ventilators, catheters, and dialysis machines.
Environmental Control: Monitoring sinks and water sources in ICUs, as they are common reservoirs.
